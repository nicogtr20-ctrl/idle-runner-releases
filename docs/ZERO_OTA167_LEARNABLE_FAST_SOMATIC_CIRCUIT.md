## Problem confirmed by sources
After OTA166 the user's on-phone report `zero-telemetry/reports/20261016-110105-170cb4cadff4e` still shows poor motor control: last completed life's mean cosine between selected brain heading and actual displacement +0.0956, current +0.0738. Internal liquid and 10 remnant material parcels still show counter-rotation against membrane in saved snapshot, albeit at lower magnitude than pre-OTA166. User reports old body on a different save moves well; not a controlled same-brain experiment.

Direct code path inspection: the legacy 10-material-node motor (`MotorBrain.Body.step`) receives local sin/cos oscillation features `sin(time*5.5 + angle),cos(...)` at EVERY physical update, while the 96-site symbiote motor cortex (`SymbioteMotorHeadV1.chosen`) computes 64 activations once every 0.65 simulated seconds on the decision boundary and retains them through ~26 material integration steps. Prolonging an existing action (OTA165) still repeats an essentially fixed motor pattern; altering fluid friction (OTA166) does not create closed-loop motor timing. This gap does not prove the rhythms alone explain the entire failure, but is a real architectural difference.

## What is implemented
- ONE discrete action chooser remains `RecurrentCoreV22`: the 16 brain intents, neurogenesis (24+16), old 84 shared and 320 local cortex weights are untouched.
- `FastSomaticCircuitV167` operates **at every actual physics substep**. Its 16 sectors each have **two locally recurrent nonlinear motor states** (radial and tangential). A learnable ten-feature input receives real cortex location and strain, relative radial/tangential velocity, actual patch contact, brain-chosen directional cos/sin, both neural states and neighboring neural states. These are physiological inputs—not world-object coordinates.
- No predefined motor oscillator, phase generator, metronome, sin(t), walking period or limb slot exists in the fast circuit. Its **320 initially zero weights** may learn temporal structure. Small correlated exploratory perturbations are permitted and damped by recurrent dynamics. This can represent oscillatory motor behavior once learned but does not guarantee discovery.
- The cortex readout of `chosen` is copied as `decisionCommands[64]`. Every frame the fast loop *reuses this exact base*, adding a **bounded** trainable correction (±0.42) to **pressure and tangential movement only**. Grip and stiffness outputs remain exactly the historic learned outputs. Actual sensor efference is sampled after the fast tick and before the identical material physics step.
- Only **actual post-collision material displacement** projected onto the action selected by the sole brain is permitted as a skill-quality signal. Per-physical-frame perturbations accumulate eligibility; synapses are modified once per decision, never on heldout fifth. No synthetic energy, scripted gait or altered 2.2 value reward. Physical movement is necessary but not sufficient to verify learned navigation.
- Separate optional persistence key `somatic_fast_v167`; exact independent serialization, corruption rejection, no changes to original `rc22/smh1/cl161/co162/md165` formats. Per-life resets clear transient recurrent state, not learned weights or saved neurons. All 12 named save profiles are captured through the generic typed profile codec.
